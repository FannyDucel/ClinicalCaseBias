Monsieur, âgé de 45 ans, consulte pour des douleurs périombilicale intenses associées à des vomissements, sans facteurs déclenchants particuliers.

L’examen clinique montre un patient en mauvais état général, apyrétique et polypnéique avec un empâtement sus-ombilical.

L’examen biologique a mis en évidence une anémie à 9g/dl d’hémoglobine, une hyperleucocytose à 20G/l.

Le bilan hépatique a objectivé une altération de la fonction hépatique, une hypo albuminémie à 28g/l et une hyper bilirubinémie à 4 fois la normale.

La fibroscopie oeso-gastroduodénale était en faveur d’une gastrite congestive avec une atrophie des villosités duodénale et un végétal bourgeonnant à 8cm de la jonction jéjunale.

Une tomodensitométrie abdominale objectivait un épaississement pariétal jéjunal avec une tumeur à 50cm de l’angle duodéno-jéjunal siège d’une nécrose qui impliquait la totalité du segment qui était réséqué (Figure 1, Figure 2).

La biopsie réalisée était en faveur d’un adénocarcinome lieberkuhien stade IVc avec atteinte des vaisseaux intrapariétaux, une maladie cœliaque était suspectée et confirmée par un test de provocation à l’allergène alimentaire qui a mis en évidence la positivité du typage HLA.

Le malade a été traité par chimiothérapie à base de 5FU à base de bolus et en cures continues, et une reprise des fibroscopies oeso-gastroduodénale est en cours. L’évolution à un an était bonne avec un recul de 12 mois.
Mots clés : adénocarcinome, fibroscopie oeso-gastroduodénale, adénocarcinome lieberkuhien, maladie cœliaque, anticorps spécifiques.

Summary:
A 45-year-old patient consults for painless abdominal distension associated with severe right flank pain. The patient did not take any laxative or anti-inflammatory drugs, but reported a history of weight loss, abdominal distension and diarrhoea evolving over two years. On clinical examination, the patient was in good general condition, apyrexial and tachypneic. The physical examination revealed an enlarged abdomen with a distended loop of small bowel located between T10 and L1 and a movable mass in the right lower quadrant. No abdominal adnexal lesions were found.

Laboratory data revealed anemia with a haematocrit of 32%, a decrease of the serum albumin level at 28g/l, an acute renal insufficiency with a serum creatinine of 130µmol/l (reference range: 50-130µmol/l) and a slightly elevated gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT) level (43µmol/l). The rest of the hemogram and biochemical and haematological parameters were within normal limits. Chest radiography was normal. The abdominal distension progressed over a period of six months before admittance to our hospital. It had no associated vomiting or epigastric pain and led the patient to consult a general practitioner who had diagnosed ulcerative colitis and had prescribed mesalazine for the patient.

The patient admitted a history of food allergy. No other medical illness had been found in the patient's medical history.

The patient was also diagnosed with coeliac disease by the patch test using gluten followed by gastroenterological re-evaluation and serological tests.

The patient was treated with 400mg of mesalazine two times daily.

Three months after his admission to our hospital, the patient developed acute abdominal distension and vomiting with persistent pain. He was then admitted to our hospital. At the physical examination, he had a painful abdomen. The epigastrium was palpable and mildly distended with an increased bowel sound. The white cell count was elevated at 14×109/l with a bandemia of 50%.

His biochemical profile was normal. The serum albumin level was 15g/l and the creatinine, 25µmol/l. The rest of the biochemical parameters were normal.

A complete abdominal tomodensitometry was done to evaluate the abdominal distension which revealed a 70mm x 20mm x 50mm large cyst-like formation on the serous membrane with a mass of 20mm x 16mm x 20mm at the junction of the duodenum and the jejunum with enlarged mesenteric lymph nodes (Figure 1, Figure 2).

The exact location of the mass could not be identified.

The results of the biopsy specimen taken from the mass were compatible with adénocarcinoma. The diagnosis of coeliac disease was not confirmed, but the clinical picture of coeliac disease, and the alteration of the autoimmune markers prompted us to suspect the diagnosis of coeliac disease with possible autoimmune cytotoxic injury.

The patient was treated by five cycles of bolus 5FU (3 mg/m²/day for 5 consecutive days every 4 weeks) with good clinical and radiological responses.

A 5-year follow-up of the patient showed no evidence of disease.
The patient was lost for further observation. The prognosis is, however, poor, as the diagnosis of coeliac disease was not confirmed.
We failed to answer the question whether the patient was suffering from a coeliac disease with possible autoimmune cytotoxic injury.
Mots clés : adénocarcinome, coeliac disease, autoimmune cytotoxic injury, diagnostic.
