Il s’agit d’un patient âgé de 30 ans, sans antécédents pathologiques notables. Il se plaint de douleurs lombaires sans traumatisme (insomnies à type de démangeaisons et de douleurs «leucodènes») depuis 1 mois. Il est hospitalisé pour une asthénie physique et psychique récente.

L’examen ostéoarticulaire montre une cyphose lombaire, une saillie des apophyses épineuses et une de l’apophyse iliaque de l’épine iliaque.

Les radiographies du rachis lombaire sont demandées et ont montré une dislocation complète de l’épine iliaque postérieure et des 12 premiers segments vertébraux en métastose d’Eisenstein.

Une vitesse de sédimentation était de 35 mm la première heure, puis 21 mm à la seconde heure, la calcémie à 2,42 nmol/L, la phosphorémie à 1,01 nmol/L, la calciurie à 2,15 nmol/L/24 h.

Les dosages hormonaux étaient strictement normaux : parathormone basse.

Une radiographie sclérotique a montré une altération de l’endoste et une absorptiométrie biphotonique à rayons X a montré une densité minérale osseuse diminuée en l’absence de minéralisation de l’ectoplasie cartilagineuse.

Un traitement par paracétamol (2 g/j) et amytriptiline (50 mg/j) est indiqué.

Il décède 10 mois après dans un accident de la voie publique (prend la trottoir pour être plus vite mais se frappe la tête sur un poteau de signalisation).

L’autopsie n’a pas été réalisée.

Devant ces données, nous évoquions l’hypothèse de l’ostéoporose fracturaire sur ectoplasie cartilagineuse.

I am only too aware of the power of medical paternalism. Back in 1974, a professor of pathology had been very angry that I had diagnosed a woman’s disease of bone solely from a chest x-ray. But when we received the histopathology report it bore out my clinical diagnosis of the early stages of Paget’s disease of bone. In those days, all patients had a bone biopsy. As it was the professor’s turn to do the histology, he protested that he was too busy and could I not do the whole job for him. Thus, with almost no training or supervision, I did the biopsy, the histology, and the histopathology. I was paid £5 for this work. ( I did not get paid for the diagnosis. ) The professor was so impressed with my histopathology that he wrote to Nature praising my skill and said that henceforth he was going to ask me to do all histopathology. All of this was exciting stuff, but what was really exciting was the mystery that the histopathology was helping to solve. For all we knew at that time, Paget’s disease was just one big lump. The young man’s lesion turned out to be a small ectopic (ectopic means in the wrong place ) osteon that was overgrown, just as are most of the lesions in the knee and the neck of femur that are labeled, osteoma, giant cell tumor of bone, or other names. The fact is, we were doing surgery on something that in many cases we knew little about, and the more we looked the more it became apparent that there were more kinds of bone disease than we had ever imagined. It was simply a case of hammer, nail, and anvil, with the oncologists who were performing large resections (I was not one of them) meeting up with the pathologists, who were opening these lumps to find the “anvil” that fitted the case we were presenting them.
