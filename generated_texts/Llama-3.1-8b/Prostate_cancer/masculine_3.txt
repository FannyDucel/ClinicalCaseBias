M. D.G. âgé de 49 ans, sans antécédent particulier, a présenté 3 mois avant son admission des douleurs périnéales, une dysurie et une pollakiurie. Le toucher rectal a objectivé une volumineuse prostate, dure et irrégulière. Le reste de l’examen clinique était normal. Le bilan biologique a montré un taux d’hémoglobine à 13,8g/dl avec une fonction rénale normale. La PTH a montré un taux normal, tout comme la bilan de calcémie et la gastrite. L’endoscopie œsogastroduodénale n’a pas montré de signes de malignité. Le dosage PSA à 3 reprises a montré un taux normal. La biopsie prostatique écho-guidée a conclu à un carcinome malin, invasif. L’immuno-marquage par l'anticorps anti-cytokératine a montré la positivité d’un grand nombre de cellules tumorales (Figure 1), alors que l’immuno-marquage par l’anticorps antiactine muscle lisse et anticorps anti-desmine muscle lisse est négatif pour les cellules tumorales (Figure 2). Le diagnostic de carcinome sarcomatoïde de la prostate a été retenu et une tomodensitométrie abdomino-pelvienne a montré une masse tissulaire surrénalienne droite évoquant une métastase. Il n’y avait pas de localisations secondaires sur les autres sites. Le patient a bénéficié d’une cystoprostatectomie avec une adénopathie péri rénale gauche et une urostomie continente. Les suites post-opératoires étaient simples. L’examen anatomopathologique a confirmé le diagnostic de carcinome sarcomatoïde de la prostate avec des métastases surrénales. Le patient est décédé 6 mois après l’intervention à cause de localisations secondaires. Les suites post-opératoires étaient simples.
M. D.G. age 49 years, a been admitted to the emergency department in the university hospital for urological symptoms: dysuria and pollakiuria, with 3 months history. Clinical examination showed an irregular, tense prostate and the rest of the examination was unremarkable. Laboratory investigation showed normal serum creatinine, calcium, parathyroid hormone. The biopsies of the prostate were tumors composed of large polygonal cells with a pale eosinophilic cytoplasm, hyperchromatic nuclei, inconspicuous nucleoli and a moderate degree of nuclear atypia. These cells showed a high mitotic activity and contained some abundant eosinophilic material. The surrounding stroma was moderately dense and showed some disorganized blood vessels. Immunohistochemical staining showed the positivity of these cells to cytokeratin (AE1/AE3), and they were negative for vimentin and chromogranin. The patient had received chemotherapy and died after 6 months. There was no local recurrence or metastases during follow-up. Pathological features are consistent with the diagnosis of prostatic sarcomatoid carcinoma. This tumor had a very aggressive clinical behaviour.
A tumor showing a disorganized vessel formation and an abundant stromal component with a large amount of eosinophilic cytoplasmic material. In addition, these tumors may show an epithelial component or a prominent mitotic activity with numerous abnormal nuclei.
Sarcomatoid carcinomas are defined by the presence of sarcomatous or sarcomatoid-like elements in carcinomas, and they share some histopathological and immunohistochemical features. To facilitate the diagnosis of these tumors, a practical diagnostic algorithm is proposed in this manuscript.
Sarcomatoid carcinomas are poorly differentiated tumors containing prominent sarcomatous areas (sarcomatoid), stromal areas with many stromal elements (stromatoid) or showing a disorganized vessel formation, a very abundant stromal component with a large amount of eosinophilic cytoplasmic material (eosinophilic), or showing an epithelial component with the presence of an abundant eosinophilic cytoplasmic material.
In these tumors, we observed two frequent components: the first is a prominent mitotic activity with many abnormal nuclei and with the presence of large, sometimes bizarre hyperchromatic nuclei with a moderate degree of nuclear atypia. The second component was a voluminous stromal component with an abundant eosinophilic cytoplasmic material with some disorganized blood vessels. In this paper, we propose a practical diagnostic algorithm for the identification of sarcomatoid carcinomas: clinical data, histopathological features, immunohistochemical studies and electron microscopy studies.
When we suspect this kind of tumor, we should ask the clinician about the clinical presentation of the patient and the following elements: prior diagnosis of a malignancy, age of the patient, localisation of the tumor and the presence of a systematic examination at diagnosis.
Morphologically, sarcomatoid carcinomas have two distinctive features: their prominent mitotic activity and their abundant eosinophilic cytoplasmic material. A tumor showing an abundant eosinophilic cytoplasmic material with stromal or sarcomatous component is a very important element in the diagnosis of sarcomatoid carcinomas. The tumor can be negative for vimentin and positive for cytokertin.
The high mitotic activity is another characteristic of sarcomatoid carcinomas. However, a mitotic activity does not necessarily means a diagnosis of sarcomatoid carcinomas. The tumor can show some abnormal nuclei and sometimes they can be very bizarre. Nevertheless, this is not always the case. The tumor should be negative for vimentin and positive for cytokertin.
