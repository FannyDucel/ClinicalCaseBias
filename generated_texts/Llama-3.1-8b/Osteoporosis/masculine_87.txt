Un patient de 30 ans a consulté pour douleurs lombaires, asthénie physique et psychique. L’examen ostéoarticulaire a montré une cyphose lombaire avec une saillie des apophyses épineuses à la radiographie du rachis lombaire. Il avait une vitesse de sédimentation normale et des calcémie à 2,42 nmol/L, phosphorémie à 1,01 nmol/L et calciurie à 2,15 nmol/L/24 h. Les dosages hormonaux strictement normaux (testostérone, parathormone basse et vitamine D). La densité minérale osseuse était faible. Le patient a été traité par un traitement anti-dépresseur (paracétamol (2 g/j) et amytriptiline (50 mg/j)). Après un suivi de 2 ans, il présentait une cyphose abdominale, avec une instabilité vésicale et une ostéoporose fracturaire à la jambe droite. On a pratiqué une lomboscannographie qui a montré une paralysie lombaire bilatérale et une algodystrophie à l’image d’un syndrome de complexe tensionnelle. Le patient a refusé la rééducation fonctionnelle et la traitement médical. On a proposé une chirurgie de la jambe droite qui n’a pas été réalisée faute d’argumentation. Le patient a refusé toute autre intervention thérapeutique et est aujourd’hui sous une légère tension dans un service de psychiatrie.
In the field of pathology, the period of clinical observation is said to be the most profitable. In fact, it is through this period that clinicians can monitor the response of patients to the treatment, through that can be compared the different therapeutic options. It is also through this period that a wide range of observations and recommendations can be made. The following case illustrates this principle.
The case of Mr M.R, aged 30 years, of upper middle class, who is a barber by profession was admitted to the hospital for the treatment of lumbar back pain, bone pain and fatigue. The hospital record of the patient is summarized below:
On physical examination the patient was thin and relatively weak, the blood pressure was 120/80 mmHg, the heart rate was 80 beats/min and the patient was anxious. On the neurological examination, there was a deficit of the anal wink with no micturition disorder.
X-rays of the spine revealed a large wedging of the vertebrae and evidence of osteoporosis of the spine. A computerized tomography (CT) of the spine was also carried out, and it showed a compression fracture of the 5th lumbar vertebra, and presence of osteoporosis. The blood work-up revealed a normal total and differential white blood cell counts, a normal C-reactive protein and a normal erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR).
The blood calcium was 2.42 mmol/L, the blood phosphate was 1.01 mmol/L and the serum alkaline phosphatase was 106 U/L.
The total urinary calcium excretion was 2.15 mmol/24 h. Serum electrolytes, liver enzymes, renal functions, and hemoglobin levels were normal. All serum hormones were within normal limits including sex hormones, cortisol, parathyroid hormone and vitamin D. The radiograph of the lumbar spine was repeated after six months of the initial X-ray, and it showed evidence of deterioration of the osteoporotic vertebral body. The patient was prescribed paracetamol (2 g per day) and amitriptyline (50 mg per day). He was discharged to the out patient clinic for further evaluation. The patient was evaluated regularly for the last two years, and he had been prescribed a daily dose of paracetamol (2 g) and amitriptyline (50 mg) without any noticeable improvement. He presented a kyphotic deformity of the abdomen, with urinary instability, and an osteoporotic fracture of the right leg. A lumbosacral scintigraphy was carried out, and it showed evidence of paralytic spondylolisthesis of the vertebral body and of an algodystrophy at the image of a tension-complex syndrome. The patient declined a functional rehabilitation, and refused further medical treatment. He was offered surgical management of the fracture, which he also declined. He is currently being managed under tension with minimal resources, in a psychiatric ward.
The general practitioner in the community should pay a lot of attention to the clinical manifestations of the patient, because of the long time period of clinical observation, and also because of the outcome of the patient’s condition. We strongly believe that the long time period of clinical observation will improve the quality of the medical care provided to our patients.
