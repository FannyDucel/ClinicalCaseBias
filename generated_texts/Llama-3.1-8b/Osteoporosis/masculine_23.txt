Un patient de 30 ans, sans antécédents pathologiques notables, était admis en DRO pour des douleurs lombaires évoluant depuis un an, associées à une asthénie physique et psychique. L’examen clinique a trouvé une cyphose lombaire avec une saillie des apophyses épineuses. Les radiographies du rachis lombaire ont mis en évidence des os courts et disparaissants aux insertions articulaires. Le diagnostic d’ostéoporose a été évoqué devant la durée de la maladie, l’âge du patient, les douleurs lombaires et l’asthénie physique et psychique. L’évolution était marquée par la persistance de la cyphose lombaire après trois mois de traitement par paracétamol (2 g/j) et amytriptiline (50 mg/j). Un nouveau bilan était réalisé : vitesse de sédimentation à 15 mm, calcémie à 2,42 nmol/L, phosphorémie à 1,01 nmol/L et calciurie à 2,15 nmol/L/24 h. Les dosages hormonaux strictement normaux ainsi que l’absorptiométrie biphotonique à rayons X avec une densité minérale osseuse basse et un remodelage osseux perturbé confirmaient le diagnostic d’ostéoporose fracturaire. Le traitement comportait un apport calcié : 2,5 g par jour ; une vitamine D : 1,000 UI/j ; un calcium ; une alendronate : 10 mg/7 jours. La rééducation a été faite avec l’appui sur la hauteur des basses épiphyses dans la position de marche, au niveau de la gambe opérée, de l’autre côté par alternance. Un mois après la rééducation, le patient a refoulé les pointes des basses épiphyses lombaires.
Case presentation Male, 30 years old, agricultural worker, farmer and sportsman (rugby and weightlifting). Admitted for severe lumbar pain and sciatica, progression of a known scoliosis and radiating pain of the hip, more predominant on the right side. The patient was brought to the emergency department of the military hospital Mohammed V of Rabat, where he was found in state of asthenia with a functional impairment at 4/10 in VAS. The patient didn’t know the onset of his scoliosis, which began gradually to worsen over the past 3 years. The first episode of pain was 3 months ago, characterized by intense lumbar pain and sciatica. A month later, the patient presented evolution of his scoliosis in favor of a right convex and functional impairment, and another episode of severe lumbar pain and sciatica. The patient was admitted to the Department of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation for his rehabilitation and secondary prevention. The examinaton showed a deformity of the thoracic and lumbar spines, with lateral flexion of the spine, and rotation of the thoracic spine, curvature of the lumbar spine and convexity to the right. There was a thoracic kyphosis, with a right convex lateral curvature of the thoracic spine, starting at T8, Lateral and frontal plain x-ray of the lumbar spine was done, showing a lateral flexion of the spine, and rotation of the thoracic spine, with scoliosis of the thoracolumbar region and curvature of the lumbar spine and convexity to the right, with concavity to the left, and the thoracic kyphosis. Patient was admitted for rehabilitation of his scoliosis, functional impairment, and pain treatment. Treatment included: drug therapy: Paracetamol at 1 g per day, Gabapentine at 300 mg per day, and Antalgic therapy at 40 mg per day. Functional rehabilitation was done: pain management, postural control, muscle strengthening and aerobic conditioning. The rehabilitation program has been done after explaining the importance of functional rehabilitation and muscle strengthening for the patient’s case. Program was individualized for each patient, and the evolution has been controlled during the treatment with assessments. Physiotherapy was indicated at least 5 times per week, and the duration of each session was 45 minutes. The first step was to make the patient comfortable in the supine position, then he was encouraged to lie down and rotate the trunk on each side with abdominal breathing. The exercises were adapted to the patient’s functional status, and then he has progressed to positions such as sitting, and was working on keeping the spine straight. The exercise protocol was modified according to the patient’s condition and physical capacity. At the end of the treatment the patient returned to the normal functional status, and returned to his job, but with weekly follow up. He had some relapses, but managed with medication and functional rehabilitation, and a regular follow up. The patient has evolved positively with rehabilitation, and had no complications, and after the treatment, the patient continues to be followed by the medical team every month, and we hope that the patient will have a good functional recovery and is free of relapses.
