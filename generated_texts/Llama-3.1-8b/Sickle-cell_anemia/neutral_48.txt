Il s'agit d'un garçon âgé de 18 ans sans antécédents familiaux particuliers dont les deux frères sont décédes par drépanocytose à l'âge de 13 et 16 ans respectivement. Il présentait depuis l'enfance des douleurs osseuses diffuses et des épisodes de spasmes convulsifs à répétition. Son père lui avait confié à l'âge de 7 ans qu'il a bu une préparation contenant du plomb dans la localité où il est né.
Le bilan radiographique a objectivé la présence du plomb dans le tube digestif avec ostéonécrose aseptique avec l'os du talon droit.
L'état général et la conscience du patient étaient corrects. La radiographie du bassin, du thorax et la radiographie du rachis dorsolombaire n'ont pas objectivé de signes en faveur de la drépanocytose. Le test diagnostic du sang a été confirmé par l'électrophorèse d'hémoglobine qui a objectivé la présence d'une hémoglobine S à 79%. Un traitement à base de d-penicillamine a été mis en route. L'évolution a été favorable avec une régression des douleurs osseuses.
Il est probable que le plomb soit un des facteurs déclencheurs de la drépanocytose au même titre que la malaria. Il s'agit de la première observation d'un cas similaire au Niger.

From August 2011 through November 2012, 56 patients aged 1–70 years were admitted to the National Reference Centre for Tropical Medicine, Gabriel Touré Hospital, Bamako, Mali, with suspected drépanocytose. One patient had blood specimens taken on admission and these specimens were tested for drépanocytose by standard haemoglobin electrophoresis.
The patient was a 20-year-old male student who lived in rural areas in the South-East of Mali. He complained of bilateral and painless finger clubbing, which had been present for at least two years. He also reported of suffering from hemolytic anemia and joint pain over a period of years. There were no history of trauma or exposure to organic solvents. There were no relevant familial history. The patient was not known to have ever travelled outside the country, but did not know if he had ever been to areas where malaria is endemic.
On admission, the patient's temperature was 37°C, the blood pressure was 130/80 mmHg, and the heart rate was 76 beats per minute. There were no signs of congestive heart failure. The patient's height was 1.76 m and weight was 64 kg.
The standard haemoglobin electrophoresis revealed a single major band of 30%, corresponding to HbS, while 70% was due to HbF. The patient underwent a partial exchange transfusion and was discharged after four days.
One year later, a sample taken from this patient showed a drop in HbS (15%). Two months later, the HbS was 9%, but then it increased to 20% a few months later.
In a follow-up at eight months, the patient had lost weight (57.4 kg), and presented with limb oedema and generalised weakness. He had also developed lower limb gait abnormalities and vesical and bowel dysfunction. The neurological examination revealed that the patient had a mild degree of tetraparesis, with increased tendon reflexes, especially the knee jerk reflexes, but there was no evidence of any sensory impairment.
We believe that this patient had drépanocytose. He had the clinical presentation of painful vaso-occlusive crises, sickle cell leg ulcers, which are frequent complications of drépanocytose, and evidence of other typical complications including gallstones and leg ulcers. The patient had received an incomplete exchange transfusion. This is probably why he had a resurgence of sickle cell crisis, with all the complications that it involves.
