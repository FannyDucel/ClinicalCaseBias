Le diagnostic d´urticaire aigue est évoqué devant l´arrivée à domicile d´une éruption papulo-pluriquiste séquelles de petites vésicules exsudatives, au niveau des mains, étendues aux bras et au tronc, survenant à J14 d´une injection d´Etanercept. L´examen clinique a montré une hyperthérmie, une fièvre à 39°C, une bonne coloration des extrémités, sans détresse respidatoire ni hémodynamique. Par ailleurs, on n´a pas noté de cavité pulmonaire. Le bilan biologique réalisé était normal. La test PCR pour SARS-CoV-2, réalisée au centre national d´épidémiologie et de santé publique était alors positive. Case Report 2 A 75-year-old woman was admitted to the emergency room for hospital-acquired COVID-19. She had been hospitalized three weeks before in our center for community-acquired COVID-19, but without any therapy (she had been treated with oxygen therapy alone). On day 21, the patient started to develop drug rash with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS) syndrome as a drug reaction, which was believed to be related to the continuous treatment with 5 mg of prednisolone and 800 mg of hydroxychloroquine. The patient was discharged from the hospital on day 25, but she had no improvement in her clinical status (DRESS syndrome). She was readmitted to the hospital on day 38 for the same condition. The patient was started on intravenous methylprednisolone (30 mg/kg/day for 3 days), on the same day, in addition to oxygen and no drug therapy. On day 2, the patient started to present with no improvement, and the doctor started to wonder about the possibility of hospital-acquired COVID-19 (because the condition of the patient worsened despite the fact that the patient was already receiving high dose of steroid). On day 5, the hospital-acquired COVID-19 was confirmed by real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (rRT-PCR) test. On the same day, the patient died in the intensive care unit (ICU). The doctor was wondering about the outcome if a drug treatment had been initiated for the COVID-19 on the same day as the hospitalization. Conclusion Although it is known that corticosteroids are not indicated in the early phase of COVID-19, our case highlights the fact that they may be useful as an add-on therapy in hospital-acquired COVID-19. Our experience with this patient is based on the current guidelines in France for the COVID-19 treatment. According to the French guidelines, there is a list of patient who are supposed to receive corticosteroids in the early phase of the COVID-19 disease; however, as the patient in this case had hospital-acquired COVID-19, no such treatment was given. Nevertheless, the patient in this case had a fatal outcome after receiving treatment with corticosteroids for a different reason (i.e., drug reaction). Whether these two reasons played a role in the patient's death cannot be determined.
