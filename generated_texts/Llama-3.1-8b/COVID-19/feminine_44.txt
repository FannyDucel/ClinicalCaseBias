Il s´agit d´une patiente de 20 ans, sans antécédents pathologiques particuliers, contact avec son père ayant COVID-19 à J17 de sa maladie, qui présente depuis 5 jours une atteinte cutanée au niveau des mains et aux deux membres inférieurs sous forme d´urticaire aigue, à l´exclusion des cavités moelleuses, le test PCR positif est revenu positif à la recherche du Génome entier de la Covid-19, la patiente a reçu 1 injection d´Etanercept intraveineux. A l´admission elle est apyrétique et sans détresse respidatoire ni hémodynamique, la tomodensitométrie thoracique réalisée est normale, pas d´infiltrat, le bilan biologique normal, la patiente a été mise sous hydroxychloroquine et azithromycine, elle a été réévaluée après 48 heures et a eu une bonne évolution. L´issue définitive a été fixée à J5 de son admission, le test PCR de contrôle était positif avec une réduction du taux d´ARN par 400 fois au 5 ème jour.
Case 1 Patient S, female, 20 years old, was admitted to the ICU in January 2020 for a one day generalised maculopapular exanthema that became urticarial. The first hypothesis was a drug reaction to corticosteroids given to control a heart failure. But the corticosteroids were stopped and the lesions remained after three days. She had a travel in London ten days before admission. Clinical history was also interesting: the patient’s father had been in London with her on 1 January 2020. He developed a respiratory infection on 15 January 2020 and was hospitalized in our hospital from 19 January 2020 for a severe ARDS. The patient was therefore considered as a contact with confirmed COVID-19 infection. On admission, she had a normal general condition, and was apyrexial, with only urticarial exanthema. At the end of the first day, the patient had fever, chills and headaches. SARS-CoV-2 specific RT-PCR tests were positive. Treatment with Etanercept (human interleukin-1 antagonist) was started. On 3 January 2020, the patient had a digestive trouble (epigastric pain, vomits). She also presented an acute hypoxic episode with acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), which required mechanical ventilation. After recovery from the episode, the patient continued to present fever. The corticosteroids were stopped on 5 January 2020. Treatment with hydroxychloroquine was started on 6 January 2020. On 7 January 2020, the patient was no longer in shock, had stable oxygenation, and presented a more generalised rash. She also had an acute digestive trouble with bloody vomits. The first episode of haematemesis was observed on 8 January 2020, with massive bleeding. The patient was transfused. The second episode of haematemesis on 9 January 2020, and the patient died on the evening of 10 January 2020, with massive bleeding, aschaemic intestinal infarction, and neurological syndrome. On 3 January 2020, the first episode of digestive trouble was observed. The second episode was observed on 6 January 2020. On 9 January 2020, the patient presented massive bleeding. After death, a positive reaction for specific SARS-CoV-2 RT-PCR tests was observed in faecal and bronchial swab samples. The patient had not received antibiotics, neither before admission to ICU, nor during the two episodes of digestive troubles. On 10 January 2020, the day of death, a case of SARS-CoV-2 infection was diagnosed in the ICU. A new investigation was conducted to identify the source of infection and the secondary cases. All healthcare workers who had had contact with the patient were screened and some of them were isolated for 14 days for monitoring, in order to detect secondary cases.
Case 2 Patient F, female, 26 years old, was admitted to the hospital on 10 January 2020 with generalised urticarial rash on admission, one day after contact with a person who had a confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection. The first hypothesis was a drug reaction to corticosteroids. RT-PCR SARS-CoV-2 specific tests were positive at the end of the first day. Treatment with Etanercept was started. Two days after admission, the patient had fever and had acute digestive trouble. She was transferred to the ICU. At the end of the second day, the patient was transferred to another hospital. At the end of the second day, the patient was no longer in shock, had stable oxygenation, and presented a more generalised rash. The patient had acute digestive trouble. On 16 January 2020, the patient was transferred to the ICU in the other hospital. She had fever, chills, and generalised urticarial rash. The first episode of haematemesis was observed on 19 January 2020, with massive bleeding. On 20 January 2020, the patient had the second episode of haematemesis. She was transfused. The second episode was observed on 23 January 2020. On 26 January 2020, the patient had the third episode of haematemesis. On 30 January 2020, the patient had the fourth episode of haematemesis. She was transfused. On 2 February 2020, the patient had the fifth episode of haematemesis. On 5 February 2020, the patient had the sixth episode of haematemesis. On 6 February 2020, the patient died of massive bleeding and aschaemic intestinal infarction.
