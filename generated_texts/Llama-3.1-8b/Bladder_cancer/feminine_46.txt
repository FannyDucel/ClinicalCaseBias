L’examen tomodensitométrique (Fig. 1) confirme la présence d’une volumineuse masse pelvienne hétérogène de 4 cm de grand axe, qui se projetait vers l’intérieur du corps vésical, prenant le rein gauche au dépend, et envahissant uniquement la graisse adjacente sans extension musculaire ou vésicale.

Il est fait une cystoprostatectomie radicale totale avec omphalectomie et entérocystoplastie de substitution. L’examen anatomopathologique montre un adénocarcinome de l’ouraque peu différencié, avec du lumen calcifié, ne montrant pas de prélèvement de cellules métastatiques. L’étude immuno-histochimique montre une positivité des cellules néoplasiques pour l’antigène Carcino-Embryonnaire et Leu-M1 et une négativité pour les cytokératines 7 et 20. La patiente est classée T III A (selon la classification de Sheldon) et ayant une performance status de 0 (selon la classification de OMS). Elle a reçu 6 cycles de chimiothérapie par Méthotrexate - Vinblastine - Endoxan -Cisplatine (MVEC) à l’intervalle de 21 jours, avec bienveillance clinique. Les suites opératoires sont simples. Ensuite, la patiente est revue en consultation annuelle. Le contrôle tomodensitométrique (Fig. 2) réalisé après 10 ans ne montre aucune échographie à l’échographie pelvienne.

A 54 years old woman is admitted to the nephrology department for the management of gross and continuous hematuria. The physical examination is unremarkable. A pelvic mass is palpable at the urinary cytometer. A Trans-abdominal ultrasound (TAUS) (Figure 1) and a thoracic-abdomino-pelvic Computed Tomography (TAPCT) (Figure 2) are performed. The CT scan confirms the presence of a voluminous pelvic tumor. The first hypothesis is that this pelvic mass could be secondary to a primary neoplasm of the urinary tract. A cystoprostatectomy is done. The macroscopic examination shows a 4 cm tumor, infiltrating the bladder, and extending toward the urethra, without any macroscopic extension to the perivesical adipose tissue. The histological examination (Figures 3 and 4) shows a low-grade grade I papillary urothelial carcinoma with little to no evidence of confluence. The lesion is infiltrating the lamina propria, but without infiltration of the muscularis propria. The edges are free of tumor and there is no lymphovascular invasion. There is no evidence of lymph node involvement. All these characteristics allowed to qualify this tumor as T2a of the classification of 2002 of the International Union Against Cancer (UICC) for urothelial tumors. The UROPATHNET test performed at the Laboratory of pathology of Dakar is negative. After cystectomy and in absence of metastasis, the patient is given the appropriate adjuvant treatments. She undergoes 2 months of chemotherapy composed of methotrexate, vinblastine, adriamycin, and cisplatin (MVA-C). After chemotherapy, the follow-up consists of a regular clinical examination and controls by CT scan. After 2 years of follow-up, the patient is without evidence of disease.

A 68 years old woman is admitted to the urology department for the management of gross hematuria. The initial examination is unremarkable. In order to achieve an etiological diagnosis, several exams are made: an ultrasound of the urinary tract and a urinary cytology. The ultrasound confirms the presence of a 2cm tumor. The cytology was negative and could not allow the diagnosis. Because of the tumor size, a transurethral resection of the tumor is done. The histological examination shows a low-grade grade I papillary urothelial carcinoma with little to no evidence of confluence (Figure 3). The lesion is infiltrating the lamina propria, but without infiltration of the muscularis propria. The edges are free of tumor and there is no lymphovascular invasion. There is no evidence of lymph node involvement. All these characteristics allowed to qualify this tumor as T1a of the classification of 2002 of the International Union Against Cancer (UICC) for urothelial tumors. The UROPATHNET test performed at the Laboratory of pathology of Dakar is negative. The adjuvant treatments is achieved with 6 months of chemotherapy composed of methotrexate, vinblastine, adriamycin, and cisplatin (MVA-C) The follow-up consists of a regular clinical examination and controls by ultrasound. After 2 years of follow-up, the patient is without evidence of disease.
Key words: MVA-C, adjuvant treatment, follow-up.
