Un homme de 45 ans, ex sportif, est hospitalisé dans un tableau de douleurs périombilicale intenses, révélatrices, associées à des vomissements de contenu bilieux avec empâtement sus-ombilical, évoluant depuis 4 jours. L’examen physique révèle une peau jaunâtre, des muqueuses conjonctivales jaunes, une anémie à 9g/dl d’hémoglobine et une hypo albuminémie à 28g/l. La fibroscopie oeso-gastroduodénale montre une gastrite congestive à diffusion dans tout le corps gastrique et une atrophie des villosités duodénale. La tomodensitométrie abdominale montre la présence d’un épaississement pariétal jéjunal de 50cm de l’angle duodéno-jéjunal, ayant rehaussé de contraste et pesant sur l’élévation de la base du pancréas. Une résection segmentaire du jéjunum 50cm après le segment duodéno-juquenal est réalisée et la microscopie montre un adénocarcinome lieberkuhien. L’étude immunohistochimique révèle que ce dernier est positif pour la CK7 et négatif pour CDX2, les suites ont montré la présence d’une maladie cœliaque sévère. Un bilan paraclinique est effectué: les lombosacrals sont positifs à la recherche de CD99, les hormones thyroïdiennes et les cortisolémies sont normales. La biopsie du lobe du foie est en faveur d’un foie cirrhotique, la radiographie pulmonaire est normale. La scintigraphie osseuse est en faveur d’une affection primitive mésenchymateuse lombosacrée. Devant le contexte clinique, biologique et histologique, la diagnose établie est d’une tumeur de kaposi dans un contexte d’une maladie cœliaque. Il est mis sous chimiothérapie et diététique et s’en est sorti. Il a été revu 1 an après l’opération et n’avait pas de récidive locale ou à distance.
Kaposi sarcoma is an uncommon tumor in adults. It is considered to be a tumor of low degree of malignancy. It usually arises in the skin but there have been several cases of the tumor developing in different sites and organs. The presence of Kaposi sarcoma in an adult without risk factor can be challenging in term of diagnosis. We present a case of Kaposi sarcoma arising in the jejunum in a middle-aged man in the context of a celiac disease. Key words : Kaposi sarcoma, celiac disease, secondary tumors, histology.
A 45-year-old man was admitted to our hospital for lower abdominal pain with vomiting and fever. The history revealed that these symptoms have been recurring in the last 2 years and they were often associated with yellowish stools. There was no evidence of weight loss, fever or other symptoms. The only associated illness was celiac disease (CD) diagnosed 2 years earlier. At that time, the patient received a gluten-free diet with a good clinical response. On admission, his general condition was relatively good, and there was no palpable lymph nodes. Abdominal examination found pain in the epigastric and hypogastric area, in the place of the mesentery, and in the right lower abdomen without guarding or rigidity. Laboratory data showed a low level of hemoglobin (8 g/dl), a low level of serum albumin (2.5 g/dl), and a very high level of alkaline phosphatase (1140 U/l). The direct and total bilirubin, and the aspartate aminotransferase and alanine aminotransferase were normal. The white blood cell count and the other labors were normal. An upper gastrointestinal endoscopy revealed erosive gastritis. A small-bowel enteroscopy was performed and it showed jejunal polyps. The large bowel endoscopy showed no abnormalities. At this stage, a CT scan of the abdomen was requested in search for primary tumor of unknown origin (Table 1). The CT revealed jejunal polyps, a large amount of abdominal fat and moderate lymphadenomegaly. A mesenteric mass was suspected. An ultrasound examination of the abdomen was normal. A jejunal biopsy was performed. At microscopic examination, many inflammatory infiltrates were present with mild active inflammation. The vili showed atrophy and villous blunting compatible with celiac disease. Kaposi sarcoma was suspected and pathologic re-evaluation of the biopsy was needed. Another CT scan was made and it showed a large mesenteric mass. At laparotomy, there were multiple nodules of Kaposi sarcoma. A segmental resection of the jejunum with lymph node dissection was performed. The patient was discharged and he was given a gluten-free diet. CD seems to occur twice as frequently in patients with Kaposi sarcoma than in the general population (1).
