Clinical Presentation:
A 28 year old male, referred to our Rheumatology clinic for evaluation of erythema nodosum and arthralgias. He presents with a 2-year history of recurrent swelling of both hands and feet with history of fevers, but no history of oral ulcers or genital lesions.

On physical examination, he is cachectic with joint swelling and erythema nodosum rash on the bilateral posterior aspect of both lower extremities. 

Both anemia and erythrocyte sedimentation rate are elevated. 

Chest X-ray demonstrates calcification of costophrenic angles.

Serologic workup reveals positive RF and ESR. 

On further questioning, patient admits to years of smoking one pack per day and exposure to asbestos dust as part of his job as an auto body shop worker.

Diagnostic Imaging:
X-rays of both knees were taken, revealing osteophytosis with joint space narrowing and subchondral cysts. 

MRI of the knees were also taken, showing similar findings to the X-ray, but with added tenosynovitis and bursitis of the infrapatellar fat pad. 

Management:
Given his history of exposure to asbestos dust and smoking, asbestos-induced arthropathy was suspected and birefringence was checked on urine sediment, which was positive.

Additional tests including CBC, ESR, and CRP were ordered.

Workup for vasculitis (ANCA, anti-CCP, RF) was also done, which was negative.

Biopsy of a skin lesion was done, which revealed chronic inflammation with aggregation of immune cells, some of which were positive for birefringence under polarized light.

Biopsy was consistent with asbestos granulomas.

Patient was started on prednisone and colchicine, which improved his symptoms, but he has had several recurrences.

Mr. E.M., 76-year-old Caucasian male, was hospitalized to our institution for traumatic hip fracture treatment. Previously he had an asymptomatic osteoporosis. In anamnesis, only arterial hypertension was described. In clinical examination there was no evidence of either fractures or congenital anomalies, no dislocations or other signs of acute traumas. No skin lesions were noted. Laboratory studies showed a marked osteopenia (T-score -4.6) and normal hematocrit.

Imaging studies consisted of X-rays and computed tomography (CT) scans of the pelvis and hip joints. Both X-rays and CT scans demonstrated a right femoral neck fracture, which was treated with intramedullary nailing. There was no evidence of neoplasia or fractures in the remaining skeletal structures.

In the course of diagnostic procedures Mr. E.M. gave a history of smoking over 20 cigarettes per day and of exposition to asbestos during his work as an electrician for over 20 years. He never had any symptoms from the respiratory system.

The pathological anatomy of the right femoral head, which had been excised together with the intramedullary nail, showed no signs of neoplasia. Radiographically and macroscopically it displayed only an osteoporotic structure. Histologically there were no signs of osteonecrosis or malignant transformation.
