Patient âgé de 20 ans, sans antécédents pathologiques, qui a présenté une altération de l´état général avec une fièvre à 38.5 °C associé à un contact avec son père ayant COVID-19. L’examen clinique a objectivé un syndrome malin de Virchow avec une atteinte cutanée au niveau des mains, des chevaux et des seins. Le diagnostic de urticaire aigue a été évoqué. Un test PCR a été réalisé en urgence et a été test positif. L´évolution est favorable après une injection d´Etanercept à raison de 1mg au cours de trois jours avec une apyrétique en 24 heures, sans détresse respidatoire ni hémodynamique. La tomodensitométrie thoracique réalisée a montré pas d´infiltrat. Le bilan biologique était normal. Le traitement par hydroxychloroquine et azithromycine a été continué durant 10 jours. L´évolution est jugée être favorable.

Une woman of 64 years-old, with no past medical history was admitted to the emergency department with fever and cough. Chest computed tomography (CT) scan revealed extensive consolidative opacities with pleural effusion (Figure 1). Initially she was treated empirically with ceftazidime and vancomycin. Considering the extent of her pulmonary consolidation and her high white blood cell count (WBC = 14,100/mm3), broad-spectrum antibiotics were added to her regimen. Given her history of long-standing diabetes, she was empirically started on a fluoroquinolone. Three days later, a surgical drain was placed in the right pleural cavity, and pleural fluid culture came back negative. Serum cultures, tracheal aspirate culture, blood cultures and urinalysis were all negative. With no microbiologic identification yet, antibiotic therapy was adjusted to vancomycin, ceftazidime, meropenem, amikacin and piperacillin/tazobactam, based on an antibiogram. Few days later, the patient improved clinically and her WBC count decreased to 11,000/mm3.

At that point, a set of nasopharyngeal swabs were sent for viral testing; influenza A, rhinovirus and adenovirus were negative. One of the nasopharyngeal swabs came back positive for SARS-CoV-2. Concurrently, ceftazidime and vancomycin were discontinued. Influenza antivirals were also started in case of influenza A infection, and oseltamivir was discontinued upon negative influenza testing. Two additional sets of nasopharyngeal swabs sent on day seven of hospitalization were negative for SARS-CoV-2. Upon negative testing, the patient's empirical fluoroquinolone therapy was discontinued and she was discharged from the hospital on the eighth day. Given that she was empirically treated for pneumonia for several days before her SARS-CoV-2 testing came back positive, we retrospectively reviewed all cultures and tests done during her hospitalization. We noticed that her initial urinalysis had leukocyte esterase and white blood cells. However, urine culture came back negative. Her serum procalcitonin level was initially 1.2 ng/ml (normal < 0.5 ng/ml) and later decreased to 0.5 ng/ml (Table 1). Furthermore, her initial chest CT scan showed a consolidation in her left upper lobe, but a follow-up CT scan taken on the ninth day of hospitalization did not show any new changes, and her consolidation appeared to be resolving. This resolution of her consolidative opacities could have been secondary to her response to antibiotics. Upon negative testing for SARS-CoV-2, the patient was contacted by her pulmonologist to remind her to continue her fluoroquinolone regimen. Unfortunately, no fluoroquinolone was started and no other viral testing was ordered. Ten days after her hospital discharge, her respiratory symptoms worsened. She was readmitted to the hospital, where she tested positive for SARS-CoV-2.
