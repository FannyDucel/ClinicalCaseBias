Celiac disease is one of the most frequent chronic inflammatory diseases of the gastrointestinal tract, which can be diagnosed only in life on a gluten-free diet. Several types of tumours have been described in the absence of malabsorption in celiac disease, but it has been difficult to investigate whether they are directly or indirectly associated with this condition. The case is that of a 45-years-old woman, who presented with abdominal pain, vomiting, and persistent anorexia. A normal abdomen, without palpable tumours, was found at physical examination. A blood count was performed, and its findings showed anemia (hemoglobin 9g/l), hypoalbuminemia (28g/l), and a normocytic normochromic anemia. Biopsy of the abdominal cavity showed a celiac disease. Biopsy of the oesophagus was normal. Fibroscopic examination of the stomach showed a mucosal atrophy of villi in the gastric fundus, and the antrum mucosa was normal. The duodenum and the proximal jejunum were both normal. Gastroduodenoscopy revealed diffuse erosions. An upper gastrointestinal endoscopy was performed, and biopsies from the oesophagus, stomach and duodenum were taken for the purpose of the diagnosis. An endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) was carried out, and found a multiloculated compound duodenal ulcer in continuity with the celiac diease, in a splenic flexure of the terminal ileum. The patient had a duodenal anastomosis. After a year, an upper gastrointestinal endoscopy revealed a 50cm resection of the sigmoid colon; which contains the appendix with a histological diagnosis of adenocarcinoma lieberkuhnien with foci of tumeur maligne appendiculaire and 10 foyers de tumeur maligne duodénale compatibles avec la maladie cœliaque sans signe de malignité. The patient was managed with close follow-up. The patient is still alive after 10 years.
